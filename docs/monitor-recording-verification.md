# Monitor recording verification

This change adds intentional monitor recordings in the private `monitor.db` SQLite database. Existing `battery.db` data remains a separate, short-lived telemetry cache; old automatic charging/discharging sessions are not relabeled as intentional recordings.

## On-device checks

1. Enable monitoring while unplugged. Open History and check that one ongoing recording appears. Open it and inspect temperature, signed power, current, voltage, remaining charge (if supported), and battery percentage.
2. Leave Fittery in the background with the screen off for 10–20 minutes, then return. Inspect sample count, observed time and recording gaps. Three seconds is a sampling target, not a guarantee while Android suspends the CPU. No wake lock was added.
3. Connect and disconnect power while that same recording is open. It should remain one monitor recording. A completed charging segment under five minutes AND below five percentage points gained should have no summary card, while its data remains in the graphs. A segment meeting either threshold remains visible.
4. Stop monitoring using both the dashboard control and the notification action. Check that the recording becomes saved. Turn monitoring back on and check that it creates a new recording. A service interruption instead preserves the open recording for resumption and marks the observation gap.
5. Rename a saved recording, filter History by a date overlapping it, open it from Settings > Past recordings, and delete it using the confirmation dialog.
6. Inspect a recording longer than an hour. Select a smaller graph range and check that the original readings and peaks remain available. History must not be limited to the current hour or ten recordings.
7. Settings defaults to 30-day expiry from each completed recording's creation. Active recordings are protected. Enable Keep recordings until I delete them to disable expiry. Turning it off permits immediate cleanup of already-expired completed recordings; cleanup also runs during history access and recording.
8. While discharging, allow at least 30 seconds for battery-life learning. Switch between idle and sustained usage: the estimate should adapt gradually, rather than following individual current spikes. A long observation gap or current-calibration change restarts learning.
9. Tap Health and inspect Settings > Diagnostics on the phone reporting 84% with 19 cycles. Collect the controller, full/design capacities, matched-pair flag and estimate source. No percentage should be inferred from remaining charge divided by battery percentage. A hardware ratio is still a firmware estimate, not independent proof of degradation.
10. Open Temperature Telemetry and leave it visible for at least one minute. The graph should update within its own element, with no repeated loading bar or container movement. Selecting another metric must not make temperature update faster. Pull-to-refresh and changing the inspected recording range should still update immediately.
11. In Settings, change Notification refresh interval between 1, 3 and 5 seconds while monitoring. Routine notification telemetry should follow that interval; charger/state changes remain immediate. Saved recording timestamps should still target three seconds, independently of notification refresh.
12. With Fittery visible and monitor mode off, connect a charger. Check the small enable-monitor dialog and the Charging sessions notification (when notification permission is allowed). Choose Later: no monitor recording should start, and ordinary battery updates should not repeat the dialog. Reconnect to receive another reminder. Enable monitoring from either the dialog or the notification prompt; the reminder should disappear and a recording should begin.
13. Unplug after recording some charging readings. Check the report-ready dialog and notification. View report from each entry point, including after reopening the app from a notification. It should open the charging time range in the saved recording, even for a brief connection. Monitor mode stays on. Full recording restores the entire recording and its summary. Unplugging without having monitored must not announce a saved report.
14. In both Telemetry and a saved recording, inspect temperature, power, voltage and current; also inspect remaining charge and battery percentage in recordings. Check Y-axis values and units, time labels, and touch/drag tooltips containing a reading and its timestamp. Signed power/current must preserve discharge values. Missing readings and observation gaps should remain blank. Check Celsius/Fahrenheit, V/mV and dual-cell settings where applicable.

## Estimation references

Android's [BatteryStatsImpl](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/7845ea4a8592ead14b1f1ce1e001b22c1bb0c746/core/java/com/android/internal/os/BatteryStatsImpl.java) estimates remaining time from observed discharge steps rather than instantaneous current. Fittery's unprivileged estimator uses the same principle of accumulating evidence, with a two-minute time-weighted current window, 30-second warm-up, ten-second estimate updates and a 90-second exponential time constant. These tuning choices belong to Fittery; they are not Android's exact algorithm.

The [Linux power supply documentation](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/power/power_supply_class.html) distinguishes remaining charge, full-charge thresholds, design capacity, instantaneous current and health status. These values are not interchangeable. Fittery requires full/design values from the same supply node for its hardware capacity ratio and does not manufacture missing health percentages from one charge-counter reading or a partial charge.
